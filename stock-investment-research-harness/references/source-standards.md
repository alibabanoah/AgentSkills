## Freshness rule

For any market-sensitive fact, browse or use a current data tool. Market-sensitive facts include price, market cap, valuation multiples, earnings dates, guidance, consensus estimates, ratings changes, regulatory decisions, macro releases, interest rates, commodity prices, and recent news.

Always state:

- `As of: YYYY-MM-DD`
- data source names or links when available
- whether figures are reported, consensus, company guidance, or analyst/model estimates

## Source hierarchy

Use sources in this order when available:

1. Primary company sources: 10-K, 10-Q, 8-K, S-1/F-1, annual report, earnings release, investor presentation, call transcript, official IR pages.
2. Market and regulator sources: SEC EDGAR, exchange announcements, FINRA, Federal Reserve, Treasury, BLS, BEA, EIA, FDA, FTC, DOJ, EU/UK/China regulators where relevant.
3. Reputable financial data/news: major wire services, exchange data pages, company-screening platforms, established financial publications.
4. Analyst or social commentary: use only as sentiment/context and label clearly.

## Preferred public sources by task

Use public, authoritative sources first. Prefer sources that publish original documents, official data, exchange disclosures, or named reporting. Do not base conclusions on rumor accounts, unattributed screenshots, anonymous forum claims, or promotional stock content.

### Company fundamentals and filings

- US-listed companies: SEC EDGAR filings, company investor relations pages, annual reports, 10-K, 10-Q, 8-K, proxy statements, S-1/F-1, earnings releases, investor presentations, and earnings-call transcripts when available.
- Hong Kong-listed companies: HKEXnews disclosures, company announcements, annual/interim reports, exchange notices, and official company IR pages.
- Mainland China A-shares: CNINFO/Juchao Information, SSE, SZSE, BSE official disclosures, CSRC materials, company annual/quarterly reports, interim reports, prospectuses, bond disclosures, equity incentive plans, major asset restructuring filings, shareholder change announcements, and official exchange announcements.
- ADRs, dual listings, holding companies, or VIE structures: check both the listing-market filings and home-market disclosures when available.

### Mainland China A-share source priority

For A-share research, use Chinese official and exchange-level sources first:

1. Company disclosures and filings:
   - CNINFO/Juchao Information for Shenzhen, Beijing, and many listed-company announcements.
   - SSE disclosure pages for Shanghai-listed companies.
   - SZSE disclosure pages for Shenzhen-listed companies.
   - BSE disclosure pages for Beijing Stock Exchange companies.
   - Company official investor-relations pages when they publish filings, presentations, investor-activity records, or earnings materials.
2. Regulators and market institutions:
   - CSRC for regulatory rules, enforcement actions, IPO/refinancing/M&A policy, penalties, and market-supervision releases.
   - SSE, SZSE, and BSE for inquiry letters, regulatory letters, discipline actions, trading halts, delisting-risk warnings, and market rules.
   - China Securities Depository and Clearing, when shareholder/settlement structure is relevant.
   - National Association of Financial Market Institutional Investors for interbank bond financing where relevant.
3. Macro, policy, and industry official data:
   - NBS China, PBOC, SAFE, MOF, NDRC, MIIT, MOFCOM, General Administration of Customs, NEA, Ministry of Transport, Ministry of Housing and Urban-Rural Development, NHSA, NMPA, and sector regulators where relevant.
   - For local policy exposure, use provincial/municipal government releases and official department notices.
4. A-share market and ownership data:
   - Exchange market data pages, official trading disclosures, block-trade disclosures, margin financing and securities lending data where available.
   - Stock Connect disclosures for northbound/southbound participation when relevant.
   - Company announcements for dividends, buybacks, lock-up expiries, pledges, reductions, private placements, and restricted-share releases.
5. Credible China financial media and databases as secondary sources:
   - Use established outlets and data providers such as China Securities Journal, Shanghai Securities News, Securities Times, Securities Daily, Caixin, Yicai, 21st Century Business Herald, Wind/Choice/iFinD summaries when accessible, and reputable brokerage research only as secondary interpretation.
   - Verify decisive claims through filings, regulator documents, exchange letters, or company announcements before using them in the thesis.

For A-shares, explicitly check:

- share class, listing board, market cap, liquidity, daily price-limit regime, trading halt history, ST or delisting-risk status
- controlling shareholder, actual controller, state-owned/private ownership, related-party transactions, pledged shares, lock-up expiries, reduction plans, and equity incentives
- audit opinion, internal-control opinion, non-standard audit language, restatements, regulatory inquiry letters, penalties, and exchange disciplinary actions
- revenue recognition, government subsidies, receivables, inventory, capitalized R&D, goodwill impairment, related-party sales, customer/supplier concentration, and cash-flow quality
- policy sensitivity: industrial policy, subsidies, procurement, price controls, medical reimbursement, real estate policy, environmental regulation, export controls, tariffs, and local-government fiscal exposure

### Market prices, valuation, and security data

- Exchange or market operator pages when available: Nasdaq, NYSE, Cboe, HKEX, SSE, SZSE, LSE, Euronext, and other relevant exchanges.
- Regulated or widely used market-data sources: Nasdaq official pages, exchange data pages, company IR stock data, major broker/data platforms available through browsing, and reputable financial-data aggregators.
- For shares outstanding, market cap, float, dividends, splits, and corporate actions, prefer company filings and exchange/company notices over third-party summaries.

### Macro, rates, inflation, commodities, and economic data

- United States: Federal Reserve, FRED/St. Louis Fed, US Treasury, BLS, BEA, Census Bureau, EIA, CFTC, FDIC, OCC, and official agency releases.
- China/Hong Kong: PBOC, NBS China, SAFE, MOF, CSRC, HKMA, HKSAR official statistics, and exchange/regulator releases.
- Europe/UK/Japan/global: ECB, Eurostat, BoE, ONS, BoJ, Japan Cabinet Office, IMF, World Bank, OECD, BIS, WTO, IEA, and national statistics agencies.
- Commodities and energy: EIA, IEA, OPEC official releases, exchange data where available, and major public commodity benchmarks.

### Policy, regulation, sanctions, trade, and legal risk

- Official government and regulator sources: White House, US Treasury/OFAC, Commerce/BIS, USTR, State Department, DOJ, FTC, SEC, FDA, EPA, EU Commission, ESMA, FCA, CMA, SAMR, CSRC, CAC, MOFCOM, NDRC, and other relevant agencies.
- Courts and legal documents when material: official court dockets, company 8-K/legal disclosures, regulator orders, consent decrees, and settlement documents.
- For sanctions, export controls, tariffs, antitrust, product approvals, reimbursement, or licensing, prefer the official rule/order/notice before news interpretation.

### Geopolitical, conflict, supply-chain, and country risk

- Official sources: government agencies, defense/foreign ministries, customs/trade agencies, election commissions, central banks, port/shipping authorities, and international organizations.
- International organizations and data sources: IMF, World Bank, BIS, WTO, OECD, UN agencies, IEA, and relevant regional institutions.
- Use major wire services and established publications for real-time event reporting only after separating confirmed facts from analysis or commentary.

### News and secondary interpretation

- Prefer major wire services and established financial publications with named reporting and corrections standards: Reuters, Bloomberg, AP, Financial Times, Wall Street Journal, CNBC, Nikkei Asia, The Economist, and equivalent reputable regional sources.
- Use analyst notes, bank research summaries, newsletters, social media, forums, or influencer posts only as sentiment inputs or idea leads. Verify core claims through primary or authoritative sources before using them in the thesis.
- When a claim appears only in one secondary source and lacks primary confirmation, label it as unconfirmed or exclude it from the main conclusion.

## Source rejection rules

Reject or quarantine sources when they are:

- anonymous tips, unsourced screenshots, chat-room claims, promotional articles, or paid stock-promotion content
- single-source claims about earnings, M&A, regulatory decisions, sanctions, lawsuits, accounting issues, or executive changes without primary or reputable confirmation
- stale summaries that conflict with latest filings or official releases
- AI-generated pages, scraped summaries, or content farms without original sourcing
- social-media narratives used as proof rather than as market-sentiment evidence

If only weak sources are available, say that the evidence is weak and do not upgrade the conclusion tier based on it.

## Evidence rules

- Separate facts from interpretation.
- Triangulate important claims with at least two sources when feasible.
- Prefer trailing twelve months, latest quarter, and management guidance over stale annual-only snapshots.
- For non-US equities, identify reporting currency, accounting standard, filing venue, and listing/share-class structure.
- For ADRs, China/HK shares, dual listings, SPACs, or holding companies, check structure, voting rights, variable interest entity exposure, and liquidity.

## Red flags requiring explicit mention

- delayed filings, auditor change, material weakness, restatement, going-concern language
- revenue recognition concerns, customer concentration, aggressive adjustments
- insider selling clusters, related-party transactions, unusual share issuance
- covenant pressure, refinancing wall, negative free cash flow with weak liquidity
- regulatory investigation, litigation, product safety issue, sanctions/export controls
- valuation dependent on distant assumptions with no current earnings or cash flow support

## Citation discipline

In final answers, include enough source attribution for the user to verify claims. If browsing is unavailable, say so and limit the conclusion to a framework or stale-data analysis.
