# Mainland China A-Share Market Strategy

Use this reference when the market is Mainland China A-shares or when the user asks about A-share strategy, Chinese domestic policy, industry rotation, or China-specific market structure.

## Principle

Do not directly apply US-style value investing to A-shares. A-share research must combine fundamentals with policy cycle, industrial policy, liquidity, valuation regime, market sentiment, ownership structure, and sector rotation.

The default A-share question is not only "is this a good company at a cheap price?" It is:

```text
policy direction -> industry transmission -> company quality -> valuation position -> capital-flow confirmation -> risk-control trigger
```

Prefer evidence-based strategy framing over slogans such as "buy cheap", "policy benefit", or "hot theme".

## A-share strategy framework

Use these strategy buckets instead of one universal value-investing lens:

1. Policy-supported quality leaders:
   - Large or mid-large companies aligned with confirmed national or local policy priorities.
   - Require real orders, capex, earnings, market share, subsidies, procurement, or regulatory support.
   - Avoid pure concept names without financial confirmation.
2. Valuation repair after risk clearing:
   - Companies or sectors where valuation is depressed, negative expectations are high, and policy/earnings/downside-risk signals are improving.
   - Require evidence that the bad news is priced in or the marginal direction is improving.
3. Industry-cycle inflection:
   - Cyclical or manufacturing sectors where inventory, capacity, pricing, exports, capex, or demand indicators are turning.
   - Require industry data and company-level operating leverage analysis.
4. High-dividend / central-SOE / cash-return strategy:
   - State-owned or mature companies with stable cash flow, dividends, buybacks, and policy support for shareholder returns.
   - Test dividend sustainability, capex burden, debt, and regulatory pricing risk.
5. Technology self-reliance and strategic substitution:
   - Semiconductors, software, industrial automation, defense, high-end equipment, AI infrastructure, new materials, and related supply chains.
   - Require localization evidence, customer adoption, margin path, R&D quality, and valuation discipline.
6. Consumption and domestic-demand recovery:
   - Consumer, healthcare, tourism, education, internet-adjacent, and services names tied to household income, policy easing, demographic shifts, or channel recovery.
   - Require demand data rather than narrative recovery alone.
7. Theme rotation / event-driven watchlist:
   - Themes driven by meetings, policy documents, reforms, price changes, M&A, restructuring, or sudden supply shocks.
   - Treat as watchlist or tactical research unless fundamentals confirm.

## Policy and news relationship analysis

For A-shares, policy can affect valuation even before earnings change. Analyze policy and news through this chain:

```text
policy document / meeting / regulator action / local implementation
-> policy priority and constraint
-> industry beneficiary or loser
-> listed-company exposure
-> expected earnings, valuation, or risk-premium change
-> capital-flow and market-style confirmation
-> trigger that confirms or falsifies the theme
```

Check relationships among policies and news:

- top-level policy vs local implementation: a central signal is stronger when local projects, tenders, subsidies, permits, or procurement follow.
- policy support vs overcapacity: support can improve demand, but excess capacity can destroy margins.
- industrial policy vs geopolitics: sanctions/export controls may benefit domestic substitution but hurt companies dependent on imported equipment or overseas demand.
- monetary/liquidity policy vs growth policy: easier liquidity can lift valuation, but weak demand may limit earnings.
- fiscal policy vs local-government constraints: project announcements may not convert to revenue if local financing is weak.
- regulation vs business model: some policy shifts support sectors while compressing margins through price controls, procurement, or compliance.
- sector rotation vs fundamentals: price strength may reflect theme funding rather than earnings revision.

## A-share policy source categories

Use `source-standards.md` for exact source priority. For strategy analysis, specifically look for:

- Central policy: State Council, NDRC, MOF, PBOC, MIIT, MOFCOM, CSRC, CAC, SAMR, sector ministries.
- Important meetings and documents: central economic work signals, five-year plans, industry plans, fiscal/monetary releases, capital-market reform measures.
- Sector rules: drug procurement and reimbursement, energy pricing, real estate financing, education rules, internet/platform regulation, environmental constraints, export controls, industrial subsidies.
- Local implementation: provincial/municipal projects, tenders, subsidy rules, industrial parks, procurement, infrastructure plans.
- Company confirmation: announcements, orders, backlog, investor-activity records, capacity expansion, customer disclosure, government grants, and accounting notes.

## Industry-chain analysis

For A-share sectors, map upstream, midstream, downstream, and policy/financing nodes:

- upstream: commodities, components, equipment, key inputs, imported dependency
- midstream: manufacturing capacity, utilization, yield, pricing, inventory, margin pressure
- downstream: end demand, customers, procurement, exports, replacement cycle
- financing: interest rates, credit availability, local-government funding, receivables, working capital
- policy: subsidies, procurement, regulation, licenses, price controls, environmental limits
- market structure: leaders vs long-tail competitors, overcapacity, consolidation, state-owned/private competition

Prefer stocks where the company sits at the profitable or strategically scarce part of the chain. Avoid companies that are "policy beneficiaries" only because they are in the same theme but have weak bargaining power.

## A-share valuation discipline

Use valuation, but adapt it to A-share market structure:

- Do not rely only on low P/E or low P/B. Cheap can remain cheap when governance, capital allocation, demand, or accounting quality is weak.
- Compare valuation with policy cycle, earnings revision, industry cycle, and liquidity environment.
- For high-dividend/SOE names, test payout sustainability and regulatory pricing.
- For technology/self-reliance names, test revenue quality, localization progress, gross margin, R&D efficiency, customer concentration, and dilution risk.
- For cyclicals, use normalized earnings and inventory/capacity cycle, not peak earnings.
- For consumer and healthcare, test demand recovery and policy/pricing risk.
- For theme names, use scenario valuation and classify as watchlist unless fundamentals confirm.

## Capital-flow and market-style checks

Before ranking A-share candidates, check market confirmation when data is available:

- sector index strength versus broad market
- northbound/Stock Connect flow when relevant
- margin financing balance and crowding
- volume/turnover change and whether it is company-specific or sector-wide
- ETF and mutual-fund style preference where visible
- daily limit-up/limit-down behavior and liquidity risk
- large shareholder reductions, buybacks, lock-up expiries, and pledge pressure

Do not use price strength alone as proof. Use it to test whether policy/fundamental logic is being recognized or whether the trade is crowded.

## A-share ranking adjustments

When ranking A-share candidates, add these factors:

- policy alignment quality: confirmed, likely, weak, or only thematic
- implementation evidence: official project, tender, procurement, order, subsidy, regulation, or company operating data
- industry-chain position: scarce/profitable node or commoditized/crowded node
- earnings confirmation: already visible, likely next 1-3 quarters, or only long-term narrative
- governance and disclosure quality
- liquidity and trading-risk profile
- valuation regime: depressed repair, reasonable growth, crowded theme, or bubble risk
- capital-flow confirmation and crowding risk

Prefer candidates where policy, industry data, company fundamentals, valuation, and market confirmation point in the same direction.

## A-share conclusion tiers

Use the normal A-D tiers, but explain the A-share strategy type:

- `Policy quality leader`: policy-supported, financially confirmed, and reasonably valued.
- `Valuation repair candidate`: depressed but risk is clearing and catalysts are visible.
- `Cycle inflection candidate`: industry data and company leverage are improving.
- `Dividend/SOE cash-return candidate`: stable cash return with manageable policy and balance-sheet risk.
- `Strategic substitution candidate`: domestic replacement leader with evidence beyond narrative.
- `Theme watchlist`: policy or market theme is interesting, but fundamentals are not confirmed.
- `Avoid`: weak disclosure, poor governance, crowded concept, high pledge/ST/delisting/accounting risk, or valuation unsupported by evidence.

## Required A-share output

When the task is A-share research, include:

- A-share strategy type
- policy and news causal chain
- industry-chain position
- company evidence from filings or announcements
- valuation and market-style context
- governance/disclosure red flags
- what would confirm or falsify the thesis
