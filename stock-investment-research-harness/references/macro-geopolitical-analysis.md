# Macro And Geopolitical Analysis

Use this workflow when current international news, geopolitics, macro policy, sanctions, tariffs, conflict, elections, supply chains, commodities, rates, FX, or cross-asset moves may affect stocks or portfolio risk.

## Principle

Do not summarize news mechanically. Build a causal research model. Convert events into an investment-risk chain:

```text
event -> transmission channel -> affected revenue/cost/financing/valuation driver -> market reaction -> scenario risk -> stock or portfolio conclusion
```

If a link in the chain is weak, state that the market connection is speculative.

The goal is to explain how policies, geopolitical events, macro variables, market pricing, sector structure, and company fundamentals interact. Treat news as raw evidence, not the conclusion.

## Deep logic workflow

Use this workflow for any task involving policy, geopolitics, macro news, or cross-market risk:

1. Establish the fact base: identify what happened, who made the decision, what is confirmed, what is rumor/commentary, and what is still unknown.
2. Identify policy intent and constraint: infer the likely objective of the policy or actor, the domestic/international constraints, and what response it may provoke. Label this as interpretation unless directly sourced.
3. Build the causal graph: connect event nodes to macro variables, sector channels, company drivers, and investor positioning.
4. Separate time horizons: distinguish immediate market reaction, 1-3 month operating impact, 6-18 month earnings/valuation impact, and long-term strategic effect.
5. Distinguish first-order and second-order effects: include direct damage/benefit and indirect consequences through competitors, suppliers, customers, inflation, rates, FX, regulation, and sentiment.
6. Test market confirmation: compare the causal thesis with prices, sector rotation, commodities, rates, FX, credit, and peer moves. Treat disagreement as a clue, not an error.
7. Find contradictions and offsets: identify facts that weaken the thesis, policy responses that may neutralize the shock, and companies whose exposure is hedged or diversified.
8. Convert to stock conclusions: update thesis, valuation assumptions, risk level, catalyst timing, or monitoring triggers only when the event changes a value driver or risk premium.

Required causal map:

```text
Policy/event -> actor objective -> likely counterparty response -> macro variable -> sector channel -> company driver -> market expectation -> valuation/risk impact -> evidence that would confirm or refute
```

Do not skip from "event happened" directly to "stock should rise/fall." Every stock-level conclusion must pass through the relevant channel and driver.

## Authority anchors

Use these sources as conceptual anchors and source categories:

- Federal Reserve and the Caldara-Iacoviello Geopolitical Risk Index for measuring geopolitical risk intensity.
- IMF financial stability and geoeconomic fragmentation work for transmission channels, capital-flow risk, stress testing, and scenario analysis.
- BIS annual and financial-stability material for tariffs, trade uncertainty, supply chains, inflation, rates, and financial-market volatility channels.
- OECD and official cyber/financial-market reports for geopolitical and cyber resilience risks.
- Official agencies and regulators for event facts: central banks, finance ministries, trade agencies, sanctions authorities, energy agencies, election bodies, securities regulators, and exchange disclosures.

## Event taxonomy

Classify each event before judging market impact:

- military conflict or escalation
- sanctions, export controls, trade restrictions, tariffs
- election or regime/policy transition
- central-bank or fiscal-policy shock
- commodity supply disruption
- shipping, logistics, or supply-chain disruption
- cyberattack or critical-infrastructure incident
- regulatory action or antitrust enforcement
- sovereign credit, capital controls, banking stress
- public-health, climate, or natural-disaster shock

## Transmission channels

Map at least one concrete channel:

- revenue: demand change, market access loss, customer spending, government contracts
- cost: energy, materials, labor, shipping, tariffs, compliance
- supply chain: input availability, lead time, single-region dependency, inventory risk
- financing: rates, credit spreads, refinancing, banking exposure, dollar liquidity
- currency: translation, transaction exposure, imported inflation, hedging cost
- valuation: discount rate, risk premium, multiple compression/expansion
- regulation: sanctions, licenses, product bans, data rules, antitrust
- sentiment/liquidity: risk-off flows, volatility, forced selling, short interest

Reject vague links such as "geopolitics is bad for stocks" unless tied to a channel.

## Interaction analysis

When multiple policies or news items coexist, analyze their relationships instead of listing them separately:

- reinforcement: two events push the same driver in the same direction
- offset: one event reduces or delays another event's impact
- sequencing: one event creates the conditions for the next event
- feedback loop: market reaction, inflation, political pressure, or corporate behavior changes the original policy path
- substitution: demand, supply, capital, or production shifts to another geography, supplier, or competitor
- contagion: stress spreads through credit, funding, FX, commodities, trade routes, or investor positioning
- concentration: several holdings or candidates share the same hidden exposure

State the dominant relationship and why it matters for valuation or risk. If the relationship is unclear, say what data would clarify it.

## Reasoning tests

Before finalizing the conclusion, run these checks:

- Channel test: Is there a concrete path from the event to revenue, cost, financing, cash flow, discount rate, or multiple?
- Magnitude test: Is the exposure large enough to matter relative to company size, margins, and valuation?
- Timing test: Is the impact immediate, delayed, temporary, or structural?
- Expectations test: Was the event already priced in, partially priced in, or a surprise?
- Alternatives test: Are there companies or sectors that benefit from substitution, reshoring, defense spending, commodity shifts, or policy support?
- Counterevidence test: What would make the causal story wrong?
- Portfolio test: Do several holdings have the same macro/geopolitical sensitivity even if they are in different sectors?

## Market confirmation

Check whether price action confirms or contradicts the event thesis:

- stock price move versus sector, market, and closest peers
- volume and volatility change
- credit spreads, CDS, bond yields, or debt price if available
- options-implied volatility or skew if available
- commodity prices relevant to the company
- FX rates relevant to revenue/cost exposure
- rates and real yields for duration-sensitive equities
- supplier/customer/competitor price moves

Do not treat price moves as proof by themselves. Use them as market evidence to compare with fundamentals.

## Exposure scoring

Score the company or portfolio:

- `Direct high`: revenue, production, assets, financing, or legal status directly exposed.
- `Direct medium`: meaningful but diversified exposure.
- `Indirect high`: strong commodity, FX, rate, sector, or supply-chain sensitivity.
- `Indirect low`: weak channel; monitor only.
- `No clear channel`: do not force a conclusion.

For portfolios, identify correlated exposure across holdings: same country, supplier region, customer group, commodity input, rate sensitivity, China/US/EU policy exposure, defense/energy/semiconductor concentration, or dollar liquidity exposure.

## Scenario analysis

Produce at least three scenarios for major events:

- base case: current facts continue without major escalation
- adverse case: event worsens through the most plausible transmission channel
- relief case: event de-escalates or policy response offsets the shock

For each scenario, state:

- affected tickers/sectors
- expected direction of revenue, margins, cash flow, valuation multiple, and risk premium
- signposts to monitor
- conclusion impact: unchanged, watchlist, risk level up, urgent review

Each scenario must explain the causal path, not only the outcome. Include the event trigger that would move the world from base case to adverse or relief case.

## News correlation discipline

When analyzing many news items:

1. Cluster news by theme rather than chronology.
2. Remove duplicate reporting and unsupported claims.
3. Separate confirmed events from market rumors and commentary.
4. Identify first-order and second-order effects.
5. Compare news impact with market reaction.
6. Update the thesis only when the event changes a value driver, risk premium, or catalyst.
7. Explain how news clusters interact: reinforce, offset, sequence, substitute, or create feedback loops.
8. Prefer a small number of high-quality causal insights over a long list of loosely connected headlines.

## Alert triggers

Escalate risk when:

- sanctions, export controls, or tariffs directly affect a company, supplier, customer, or market
- conflict threatens production, logistics, energy, shipping lanes, or insured assets
- commodity or FX move materially changes margin assumptions
- rate or credit shock changes refinancing risk
- election or policy shift changes subsidy, tax, pricing, reimbursement, or licensing assumptions
- market reaction is severe and company-specific relative to peers
- cross-asset signals show rising stress while equity price has not adjusted
