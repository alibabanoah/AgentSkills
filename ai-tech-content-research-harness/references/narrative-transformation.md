# Narrative Transformation

Use this reference to turn verified AI tech information into compelling self-media content.

## Core transformation

Transform raw information through:

```text
fact -> conflict -> mechanism -> human impact -> takeaway
```

Do not stop at "what happened." Explain "why this matters now" and "what changes for the audience."

## Common narrative structures

### 1. Hidden mechanism

Use when the source is a product, policy, paper, or company move.

```text
Everyone saw [surface event].
But the real signal is [hidden mechanism].
This matters because [audience impact].
The next thing to watch is [trigger].
```

### 2. Big-person signal

Use when a founder or investor speaks.

```text
[Person] said/did [event].
Most people read it as [surface meaning].
But in his/company strategy, it points to [deeper direction].
For ordinary people, the question is [impact].
```

### 3. Ordinary-person crisis

Use for education and career content.

```text
AI is not replacing your job title first.
It is replacing the training path that used to make you valuable.
The danger is not only unemployment; it is losing the ladder from beginner to expert.
```

### 4. Creator opportunity

Use for AI creative cases.

```text
The impressive part is not that AI made a video.
The impressive part is that one person used taste, iteration, and tool orchestration to replace an old production team.
```

### 5. Capital logic

Use for investor and company trends.

```text
Capital is voting with money.
It is leaving [old bottleneck] and entering [new scalable system].
That tells us which human skills are becoming cheaper and which are becoming more valuable.
```

## Strong hook patterns

Use hooks that are intense but fact-safe:

- "This looks like a tool update, but it is actually a career signal."
- "The scary part is not what AI can do. It is which training path it quietly removes."
- "A small creator just proved that the production bottleneck has moved."
- "This founder statement is easy to dismiss, but investors are already acting on it."
- "If this trend continues, the most valuable person is not the one who knows prompts, but the one who knows the business."

## Content depth checklist

Include:

- one concrete case
- one verified source or source type
- one deeper mechanism
- one audience implication
- one practical takeaway
- one uncertainty or watch point

## Style handoff

After choosing the narrative structure, read `secondary-creation-stylebook.md` when the user needs a specific voice, platform draft, rewrite, short-video script, Xiaohongshu note, WeChat article, Bilibili script, or more attractive secondary creation.

Narrative structure answers:

```text
What is the story logic?
```

Secondary creation style answers:

```text
How should this be expressed so people want to watch or read it?
```

## Avoid shallow formats

Avoid:

- list of news without thesis
- fear without mechanism
- hot words without example
- "AI changes everything" without explaining what changes
- moral judgment without evidence
