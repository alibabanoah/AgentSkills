# Secondary Creation Stylebook

Use this reference when transforming verified AI technology information into self-media articles, short videos, Xiaohongshu notes, WeChat posts, Bilibili scripts, newsletter items, or commentary.

## Principle

Secondary creation is not rewriting words. It is rebuilding:

```text
source fact -> audience problem -> emotional entry -> explanatory mechanism -> creator viewpoint -> practical takeaway
```

Make the content more attractive by changing angle, rhythm, framing, examples, and audience relevance. Do not change facts, numbers, dates, quotes, causality, or certainty.

## Style selection

Choose one primary style and one secondary style. Do not mix too many voices.

### 1. Sharp commentary

Use for controversial tech-founder statements, capital moves, education/job shocks, and public debates.

- voice: direct, critical, intellectually aggressive
- opening: contradiction or challenge
- best for: short videos, opinion posts, WeChat commentary
- avoid: personal attacks, unsupported accusations

Pattern:

```text
Most people are arguing about [surface issue].
But the more important question is [deeper conflict].
If [trend] continues, the real loser/winner may be [audience/group].
```

### 2. Calm deep analysis

Use for complex AI industry changes, company strategy, product launches, papers, and regulation.

- voice: restrained, structured, evidence-heavy
- opening: "This looks like X, but it is actually Y"
- best for: WeChat articles, Bilibili, long-form scripts
- avoid: over-dramatizing or turning every topic into crisis

Pattern:

```text
This event has three layers.
The first layer is [fact].
The second layer is [industry mechanism].
The third layer is [impact on ordinary people/business].
```

### 3. Ordinary-person translation

Use when the source is technical, institutional, or abstract.

- voice: plain, explanatory, empathetic
- opening: explain why the audience should care
- best for: Xiaohongshu, short video, public account education posts
- avoid: jargon stacking

Pattern:

```text
You do not need to understand every technical detail.
You only need to understand one thing: [plain-language mechanism].
This may affect [learning/work/creation/business] because [reason].
```

### 4. Crisis warning

Use for job displacement, education inequality, entry-level role collapse, privacy, safety, and governance topics.

- voice: serious, urgent, but not panic-driven
- opening: hidden risk or overlooked cost
- best for: short video, WeChat, Bilibili commentary
- avoid: "everyone is doomed", deterministic fear, unsupported numbers

Pattern:

```text
The scary part is not [obvious fear].
The scary part is [hidden mechanism].
Because once [training path/resource/channel] disappears, [audience] loses [specific opportunity].
```

### 5. Opportunity playbook

Use for new AI jobs, creator cases, workflow changes, business transformation, and skill-building content.

- voice: practical, optimistic, strategic
- opening: new opportunity or skill mismatch
- best for: Xiaohongshu, career posts, short video, private-domain content
- avoid: selling unrealistic shortcuts

Pattern:

```text
The opportunity is not [surface tool].
The opportunity is [new capability].
People who can [business/context/taste/workflow] will benefit more than people who only [basic tool use].
```

### 6. Founder/investor signal decoding

Use for Musk, Altman, Huang, Zuckerberg, VC memos, funding rounds, and earnings-call remarks.

- voice: strategic, interpretive, slightly dramatic
- opening: famous-person signal
- best for: short video, newsletter, WeChat
- avoid: treating every statement as prophecy

Pattern:

```text
[Person] did not just say [surface statement].
He is pointing to [strategic direction].
The key is not whether you agree with him, but whether capital and products are moving in the same direction.
```

### 7. Case-story narrative

Use for AI creator breakthroughs, startup stories, education pilots, viral demos, and tool workflows.

- voice: story-first, concrete, visual
- opening: one surprising case
- best for: short video, Xiaohongshu, Bilibili
- avoid: making one case universal proof

Pattern:

```text
A [person/team] used [AI/tool] to do [result].
The impressive part is not [surface result].
The real signal is that [old bottleneck] is disappearing.
```

### 8. Reverse-consensus angle

Use when public discussion is one-sided or shallow.

- voice: contrarian but evidence-based
- opening: "Most people got this wrong"
- best for: opinion pieces, short video hooks
- avoid: contrarianism for its own sake

Pattern:

```text
Most people think this is about [popular explanation].
I think it is more about [deeper mechanism].
The evidence is [fact 1], [fact 2], [fact 3].
```

### 9. Methodology teardown

Use when teaching how to think, write, use AI, build workflows, or understand industry logic.

- voice: instructional, clear, stepwise
- opening: mistake or misconception
- best for: tutorials, career content, creator education
- avoid: abstract frameworks without examples

Pattern:

```text
Do not copy the result. Copy the process.
The process has three steps: [step 1], [step 2], [step 3].
```

### 10. Humanistic reflection

Use for education fairness, children, labor dignity, creativity, human value, and AI ethics.

- voice: warm, reflective, restrained
- opening: human consequence
- best for: WeChat, long video, thoughtful posts
- avoid: empty moralizing

Pattern:

```text
Technology is solving [efficiency problem].
But it also forces us to ask [human question].
The answer cannot be only cheaper, faster, and more automated.
```

## Expression intensity levels

Choose an intensity level:

- Level 1: neutral explanation. Use for uncertain or technical topics.
- Level 2: clear viewpoint. Use for normal commentary.
- Level 3: strong contrast. Use for social impact, jobs, education, and founder signals.
- Level 4: high tension. Use only when facts are strong and stakes are concrete.

Never use Level 4 when the source is rumor, a single screenshot, a clipped quote, or an unverified statistic.

## Rewrite workflow

When asked to create secondary content:

1. Identify the verified fact.
2. Choose target audience and platform.
3. Select style and intensity.
4. Build the content spine:
   ```text
   hook -> case/fact -> conflict -> mechanism -> audience impact -> takeaway -> watch point
   ```
5. Add platform packaging.
6. Run fact-risk check.
7. Output final draft plus risk notes when needed.

## Language techniques

Use:

- contrast: "not X, but Y"
- compression: reduce complex tech to one clear mechanism
- escalation: show why a small signal points to a larger trend
- personalization: "what this means for students/parents/creators/PMs/founders"
- concrete examples: one product, one person, one company, one job role
- restrained metaphor: "ladder", "infrastructure", "training path", "production bottleneck"

Avoid:

- empty buzzwords: revolution, disruption, epoch-making, unless explained
- exaggerated certainty: must, definitely, everyone, all jobs
- fake insider tone: "the truth no one dares say"
- moral panic without evidence
- overusing famous names when the topic does not depend on them

## Final quality checklist

- Is the style chosen deliberately?
- Is the hook attractive within factual boundaries?
- Is there a clear mechanism beyond news summary?
- Is the audience impact specific?
- Is there at least one concrete example?
- Are uncertain claims softened?
- Is the ending useful rather than only emotional?
