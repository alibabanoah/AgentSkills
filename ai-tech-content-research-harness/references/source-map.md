# Source Map

Use this reference to collect AI technology, frontier innovation, education, career, creator-economy, tech-founder, investor, and company information.

## Source hierarchy

Use sources in this order:

1. Original sources: company blogs, product pages, research papers, arXiv, GitHub repos, official demos, filings, conference talks, full interviews, podcasts, and founder posts.
2. Institutional sources: universities, labs, think tanks, standards bodies, regulators, public agencies, official statistics, and reputable research institutes.
3. Reputable reporting: named reporting from established technology, business, science, and financial media.
4. Platform signals: X/Twitter, YouTube, Bilibili, Xiaohongshu, Reddit, Hacker News, Product Hunt, GitHub trending, Hugging Face, Discord communities, WeChat public accounts, and creator newsletters.
5. Commentary and influencer interpretation: use as leads, not proof.

## Viral case sources

For creator stories and viral cases:

- find the original creator account or work
- verify publication date, tool stack, budget, timeline, and actual audience response
- distinguish creator self-report, media reporting, and third-party proof
- check whether "company collaboration", "Hollywood praise", or "investment interest" is confirmed

## Source rejection and quarantine rules

Reject or quarantine:

- anonymous screenshots without original link
- clipped quotes without full context
- translated summaries that do not link to the original
- AI-generated listicles or scraped pages without sources
- promotional tool posts that exaggerate capability
- single-source claims about job loss, layoffs, funding, lawsuits, safety incidents, or medical/education outcomes
- "everyone is saying" social narratives without identifiable evidence

If a topic is attractive but weakly sourced, keep it as a lead and produce a verification plan instead of a final content claim.

## Collection discipline

For every lead, capture:

- title or event
- source URL or source name
- date
- source type
- core fact
- why it matters
- what needs verification
- possible content angle
